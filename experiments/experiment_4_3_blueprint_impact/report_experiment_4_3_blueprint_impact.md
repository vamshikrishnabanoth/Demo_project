## 1. Executive Summary & Experimental Context

Experiment 4.3 investigates the central research inquiry of Phase 4:
> **Central Research Question:** *Does conditioning assessment planning on observed pedagogical behaviors produce measurable, behavior-consistent adaptations in assessment blueprints, and do those adaptations propagate downstream into question quality?*

### Overarching Finding:
> *Phase 4.3 demonstrates that injecting observed teaching-behavior information can measurably alter assessment planning while preserving grounding in the evaluated legitimate conditions. The effect on blueprint alignment was sparse because baseline alignment was already high in most packages. The permuted-control condition demonstrated sensitivity to mismatched behavioral information and produced three downstream grounding failures, providing evidence that behavior payloads can influence downstream assessment generation. C5 showed greater stability than C4 in this evaluation, particularly in target yield and grounding compliance, although C4 achieved higher mean teaching-context alignment. These findings support further production validation of behavior-conditioned planning but do not establish universal generalization or production superiority.*

Across a six-package instructional corpus spanning three instructional styles (Git Fundamentals, Recursion Algorithms, and Deep Learning Latent Modeling) and five experimental conditions (**Condition A: Baseline Agnostic**, **Condition B: Oracle Ground Truth**, **Condition C: C4 Zero-Shot**, **Condition D: C5 Few-Shot Calibrated**, and **Condition E: Permuted Cyclic Derangement Control**), Experiment 4.3 evaluated:

### 4.2 Mathematical Derivation of Identical Cohen's $d_z = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}$
Conditions B, C, and D each yielded identical paired effect sizes: $d_z = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \approx 0.408248$. This identity is a mathematical consequence of the paired data structure across the $N=6$ instructional packages:
* For each condition $X \in \{B, C, D\}$, exactly one package (`pkg_02_git_workflow`) experienced a non-zero alignment improvement ($\Delta_2 = +0.40$ for B; $\Delta_2 = +0.20$ for C and D), while the other five packages experienced zero change ($\Delta_i = 0$ for $i \in \{1, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$).
* Because the paired difference vector $\Delta$ is a positive scalar multiple of the elementary basis vector $e_2 = [0, 1, 0, 0, 0, 0]^T$:
  $$\bar{\Delta} = \frac{k}{6}, \quad s_\Delta = \sqrt{\frac{1}{5} \sum_{i=1}^6 (\Delta_i - \bar{\Delta})^2} = k \sqrt{\frac{1}{6}}, \quad d_z = \frac{\bar{\Delta}}{s_\Delta} = \frac{k/6}{k / \sqrt{6}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \approx 0.408248$$
The effect size is mathematically invariant to the positive magnitude $k$ whenever exactly one package improves and five remain unchanged.

## 8. Evaluation of Primary Hypotheses ($H_1$–$H_4$)

* **$H_1$ (Behavioral Alignment):** Supported in Sparse Form. Behavioral conditioning produced measurable alignment changes, but the effect was sparse because baseline $BDAS$ was already high in most packages. In `pkg_02_git_workflow`, alignment improved by 40 percentage points ($0.60 \rightarrow 1.00$). However, on 5 of 6 packages, baseline alignment was already high ($0.80$–$1.00$), resulting in directional counts of 1 higher, 5 equal, 0 lower across B, C, and D, and an identical paired effect size $d_z = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \approx 0.408$.
* **$H_2$ (Pedagogical Directionality):** Descriptively Supported. Behavior-conditioned blueprints showed package-specific shifts toward assessment dimensions compatible with the injected behaviors, including increased use of Comparison/Tradeoff and Flow/Trace in relevant packages (e.g. `pkg_02` and `pkg_06`), without assuming higher Shannon entropy is universally desirable.
* **$H_3$ (Grounding Preservation):** Supported in Evaluated Sample. No grounding degradation was observed in the evaluated behavior-conditioned conditions: **116/116 generated questions passed the deterministic Grounding Gate** across Conditions A, B, C, and D. In contrast, Condition E produced 3 grounding failures among 30 questions, all associated with mismatched behavioral conditioning and foreign-topic drift.
* **$H_4$ (Signal Sensitivity & Discrimination):** Supported with Empirical Granularity. The permuted-control condition demonstrates sensitivity to the semantic content of the injected behavior payload; it does not by itself establish that the planner correctly understands the underlying teacher behavior. Human Oracle conditioning served as an interpretable human-reference condition, driving the highest generated alignment ($0.967$). C5 showed the more stable behavior-conditioning profile in this evaluation, maintaining full target yield ($30/30$), perfect stem clarity ($D_3 = 5.000$), and 100% grounding compliance, while C4 achieved higher mean teaching-context alignment ($4.593$ vs $4.367$) but exhibited a substantial target-yield deficit in one package ($2/5$ targets). These results support C5 as a candidate for further production-oriented validation, rather than establishing production superiority.

## 9. Key Scientific Conclusions & Architecture Implications

1. **The Primary Locus of Behavioral Conditioning is Assessment Planning (Agent 1):**  
   Conditioning on observed teacher behaviors alters assessment target selection—shifting targets toward live demonstrations, comparisons, and execution flows where warranted by lecture evidence.
2. **Question Generation (Agent 2) is Strongly Constrained by Generation & Gating Architectures:**  
   Once targets and evidence quotes are selected, question-level execution (stem clarity $D_3 \ge 4.78$, determinism $D_5 \ge 4.60$, and distractor plausibility $D_4 \sim 4.10$) remains tightly governed by the underlying LLM generation prompt and the deterministic Grounding Gate.
3. **C5 as a Candidate for Production-Oriented Validation:**  
   C5 few-shot calibration demonstrated greater operational stability than C4 zero-shot, avoiding yield collapse while maintaining full grounding compliance. These findings support C5 as the primary candidate for future production consideration.
4. **Absolute Production Isolation Preserved:**  
   All Phase 4 experiments were executed in strict lab isolation. Production release `v3.4-frozen` remains intact and reproducible.
